# DM Motion Story Playbook, v2 (Kit reels)

## Rule zero: quality over speed, always
Never compromise quality for speed, for any reel, ever. A reel is finished only when every part of the whole video is at a standard of excellence: background, midground and foreground with depth; props drawn so they are recognizable and physically make sense (held, resting, attached, never floating); characters with natural movement (breathing, blinks, weight shifts, arms doing what the action needs); lighting and shadows; sound tied to the beats. Nothing is rushed. Check every scene at full size before rendering, and watch the whole render before delivering. If a number of reels is requested for a day, state only the honest time it takes to make all of them at this standard. Reels get posted only when they meet it.

Purpose: every reel is a true, verified story, told with top-tier pixel graphics. The ADA rule is the payoff, never the opening.

## B. The Kit Story Spine (35-50 s, about 100 words)

| Beat | Time | Job | Research |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook | 0-3 s | Start near the end: the injustice + a strange pairing. One gap. Cover shows the hidden hero number ("?? DAYS"). | 5, 10, 13 |
| 2 Who | 3-9 s | One named, real person and what they wanted. A concrete, picturable detail. | 2, 4, 11 |
| 3 But | 9-16 s | The obstacle. Indignation. | 3, 9 |
| 4 Therefore | 16-22 s | What they did. Action, not summary. | 9, 11 |
| 5 Re-hook | 22-28 s | Stakes escalate (worse than expected). New visual spectacle. | 1, 12 |
| 6 But (turn) | 28-34 s | Unexpected help or twist. | 1, 3 |
| 7 Climax | 34-40 s | The hidden number lands huge on screen. Win. Awe. | 5, 6 |
| 8 Payoff | 40-46 s | The one rule it led to, as a picture, plus one reflective line. | 3, 11 |
| 9 Out | last 2-3 s | "Save this. Free cheat sheet in my bio." Cut. No lingering. | 12 |

## C. Script rules
1. True and sourced: named person, date, place, and a source for each fact. No invented quotes. No causal claims the sources don't make.
2. One protagonist. One gap. One hero number.
3. Every line connects with "but" or "therefore". Delete any line that is just "and then".
4. Concrete actions and picturable details over summaries.
5. Arc: fall then rise. Emotion: indignation then awe. Never end sad.
6. At least 3 moments of change.
7. The hook's promise is paid off exactly.
8. Under ~110 words. Read it aloud; cut anything a 12-year-old wouldn't follow.

## D. Visual rules (equal to the story)
1. Every beat is a new picture: new scene, camera move, or Kit reaction. Never hold a static frame through two lines.
2. Show what the VO doesn't say (closure). Captions carry key words only.
3. Cold-open cover: Kit visible, big hero question, hidden number, centered with even margins.
4. Depth layering, scale and thorough animation as before. Kit is the viewer's stand-in who witnesses the story.
5. Sound: silence before the climax, a hit on the reveal, music lifts at the win.
6. Real people are drawn respectfully and generically, not as caricatures.

## D2. Pre-render checklist (set in stone; run before every render)
1. Direction: every character faces the way it travels, always. Wheels spin the way the chair moves; arms push the way the chair moves. Run `motioncheck.py <reel.html> <duration>`; the only allowed hits are scene cuts.
2. Holding: anything carried shows the hands on it (sign poles, crates, bags, papers). Nothing floats.
3. Resting: every prop sits on a surface or hangs from a fixture. Shadows under things that stand on the floor.
4. Path of travel: nothing stands in a character's path unless the story puts it there; characters are never half-hidden behind furniture unless that is the point, and then the overlap must read cleanly.
5. Scale: doors taller than people; furniture at human scale; one consistent pixel scale per scene.
6. Camera: if the camera moves, every layer moves with its own parallax, including the characters.
7. Idle life: nobody freezes while a line plays (breathing, blinks, small shifts).
8. Full-size stills from every scene, then a frame every half second across the whole render, viewed before delivery.

## E. Process
1. Find the story (person, injustice, action, twist, win, rule). Verify each fact.
2. Script to the spine; test against the rules above.
3. Storyboard (one frame per beat) and check stills.
4. Build, render, watch the whole thing, fix.
5. Post only when ready. 24 h later: skip rate, average watch %, sends, saves. Keep what works, change one thing at a time.
